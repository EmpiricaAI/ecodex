The difference

Most "AI compliance" is a document. Ours is a command's return value.

The binder

A policy PDF asserts a control exists. It's true the day it's signed and drifts every commit after. Nobody re-runs a PDF. The gap between the attestation and the code is invisible until an audit — or an incident — finds it.

vs
The check

empirica compliance-report runs the deterministic check set and emits the regulatory mapping as JSON. If a check fails, the matching claim is withdrawn automatically. The posture can't drift out of date — it's recomputed, not remembered.

Where the substrate IS the control

The hard articles aren't features bolted on. They're how the system runs.

Anyone can ship a linter. The distinctive rows are the ones where the control mechanism is the epistemic loop — auditability, oversight, and explanation of automated decisions become structurally unavoidable.

Record-keeping

Every PREFLIGHT→CHECK→POSTFLIGHT cycle is a timestamped, git-notes-anchored record of what the AI knew, decided, and did. The audit trail isn't generated for the auditor — it's the runtime.

EU Art. 12 GDPR Art. 30
Right to explanation

Decisions are logged with rationale and reversibility as first-class, queryable artifacts. The "why" of an automated choice survives the session that made it.

EU Art. 13 GDPR Art. 22(3)
Human oversight

The Sentinel measures the AI's self-assessment against deterministic evidence; divergence is the oversight signal. Consequential action is ECO-gated — a human sits on the critical path by construction.

EU Art. 14
AI disclosure & provenance

Commits carry AI co-authorship; artifacts carry an epistemic_source tag (intuition vs. search). Provenance is recorded at write time, never reconstructed after.

EU Art. 50 ISO A.8.4
ecodex-native · controls on the controls

ecodex hardens the layer that enforces compliance — so it can't silently rot.

As a fork of openai/codex on the Empirica substrate, ecodex adds determinism core has no analog for. Two of these guard the governance layer itself: a dead audit-log query or a slower firewall is a compliance regression that looks green.

SQL schema-reference guard #1

Every static query the governance hooks issue is validated against the real schema at test time. A silently-dead audit or firewall query fails CI instead of no-op'ing in production.

EU Art. 15(1) EU Art. 12* ISO 8.5
Import-budget gate #3

The per-tool-call hook spawn — the Sentinel firewall — stays free of heavy eager imports. Bounds the latency of the control that enforces the other controls.

EU Art. 15(3) ISO 8.4
Rust spawn / sandbox boundary

Tool execution crosses an explicit sandbox + writable-roots boundary before any hook or model action — an enforcement point a Python CLI has no equivalent for.

EU Art. 15(4) GDPR Art. 25
Model-routing transparency

Which model served a turn is explicit provider config, not hidden. The disclosed-AI surface names the actual model behind each response.

EU Art. 50 EU Art. 13
Guards #1 and #3 don't satisfy an article about the product. They guarantee the mechanisms that do — the audit trail, the oversight firewall — cannot silently fail. That turns "the record-keeping path works" into a tested claim, not a hopeful one.
* Art. 12 mapping is indirect: the guard protects the integrity of the record-keeping path, not the record itself.

▼ in progress

This page is the human-readable rendering of what those commands produce. If a row's check fails, the regulatory claim is withdrawn automatically — which is the whole point: compliance posture is computed, never signed.

Scope honesty: this is a crosswalk, not a certification. "Maps to Art. X" means "this check is evidence toward that article's intent," not "ecodex is certified under Art. X." Conformity assessment is a legal process; this is the engineering substrate that makes one tractable.
